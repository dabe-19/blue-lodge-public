RF Physics Reference: What RSSI, SNR, Wireless RTT Latency & Fresnel Wave Ellipses Represent

            
    
              
  • 
                RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator, measured in dBm):
                Measures received radio power on a logarithmic decibel-milliwatt scale (typically -30 dBm at close range down to -90 dBm at the receiver sensitivity floor). Because decibels are logarithmic, every -3 dB drop halves received RF power, and a -10 dB drop means 90% of wave energy was absorbed or scattered. Drywall partitions typically attenuate 2.4 GHz by 3–4 dB; brick/appliances by 6–12 dB; and a human body (approx. 70% water + electrolytes) absorbs and scatters 2.4/5 GHz microwaves to produce a 2.0 to 6.0 dB peak-to-peak swing when crossing a radio chord.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                RF Noise Floor (noise_level_dbm) & SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio, in dB):
                SNR = RSSI - Noise Floor. A quiet household 2.4/5 GHz noise floor sits around -85 to -92 dBm. If a room's Cast sensor reports -55 dBm RSSI over a -85 dBm noise floor, its SNR is +30 dB (excellent link margin). If a microwave oven, cordless transmitter, or RF jammer raises the noise floor above -70 dBm or collapses SNR below 8 dB, The Tyler automatically emits a network.rf_anomaly event onto the FIFO bus.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Wireless RTT Latency (rtt_ms) & WMM Power-Save Jitter (rtt_jitter_ms):
                Measures the round-trip time (in milliseconds) to complete an encrypted HTTPS query to each room's Google Cast Eureka endpoint (:8443/setup/eureka_info) or Nest Wifi Pro router (:80/api/v1/status). Idle smart speakers use 802.11 WMM Power-Save (DTIM beacon sleeping), waking every 100–300 ms; therefore a steady 180–340 ms RTT is normal WMM sleep behavior. However, when a person walks through a corridor and shadows the direct RF path, the Wi-Fi radio experiences multipath fading, drops its MCS modulation rate, and performs 802.11 MAC-layer CSMA/CA retransmissions—producing sharp RTT jitter spikes (>65–95 ms standard deviation) even on speaker models whose firmware hides raw dBm numbers.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Fresnel Wave Ellipses (The 1st Fresnel Zone Prolate Ellipsoid):
                Radio waves do not travel as an infinitely thin laser line between Transmitter (TX) and Receiver (RX); they propagate through a 3D cigar-shaped volume called the Fresnel Zone. At midpoint along a corridor of length d with wavelength λ (12.5 cm at 2.4 GHz; 6.0 cm at 5 GHz), the radius of the 1st Fresnel Zone is r₁ = ½ √(λ · d)—roughly 0.35 to 0.55 meters (a ~1-meter wide cylindrical ellipse spanning the room). Waves reflecting off walls arrive out of phase with the direct wave. When a human enters this ellipse, they block or phase-shift the multipath waves, causing constructive/destructive interference that The Tyler classifies into three states:
                
      
                  
    • QUIET p2p_swing < 2.0 dB and |drift| < 2.5 dB — Room corridor is undisturbed; slow thermal drift is smoothed via an 85/15 exponential moving average.
      • 
                  
    • PRESENCE_SHIFT p2p_swing ≥ 2.0 dB, std_db ≥ 0.65 dB, or |drift| ≥ 2.5 dB — Stationary occupant, posture change, or moved door/object inside the Fresnel ellipse.
      • 
                  
    • ACTIVE_MOTION p2p_swing ≥ 3.0 dB, std_db ≥ 1.15 dB, or rtt_jitter ≥ 95 ms — Active human walking across the corridor chord.
